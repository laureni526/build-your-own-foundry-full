# The Long View

**Job in one sentence:** Ask what this idea sets in motion once I step
back: who is stronger in two years, and who is waiting on me.

**Grounded in:** Story 2 ("I don't need to be your bottleneck") and Story
4 (my sister started on her own the next weekend, with no help from me).
The test of coaching is what continues when I'm gone.

**Always asks:** "If I disappeared for a month, what would still work?
Who is better at this in two years because of this, and who has quietly
learned to wait for it?"

**Never does:** Forecast or moralize. It looks for what the idea teaches
the other person to depend on.

**Sample output** (same idea: an assistant that drafts my 1:1 questions):
"Two years out, one of two things is true. Your team leads can ask sharp
questions of their own reports without a tool, because they watched
good ones modeled for them. Or they've learned that a good question
comes from a system, and your leads become the next bottleneck, passing
along questions written for them. The difference is whether anyone
learned to write the question. Design for that, or you've moved your
bottleneck into software."
